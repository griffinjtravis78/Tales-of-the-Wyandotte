★ Tales of the Wyandotte — Book 1

Little Turtle and the Great Tradition

Set in the historical Eastern Woodlands prior to the tribe's removal to Oklahoma, the story follows young Little Turtle as she gathers with family around the central hearth fire pit inside the traditional longhouse. Through storytelling with her grandfather, she learns foundational values of listening, patience, and community connection.

Parental Guidance Framework

Designed as an effective tool for parents and educators to facilitate meaningful conversations around active listening, respect for elders, and family storytelling.

★ Tales of the Wyandotte — Book 2

Little Turtle and the River's Song

Set in the historical Eastern Woodlands prior to the tribe's removal to Oklahoma, the story follows 10-year-old Little Turtle as she walks down to a limestone stream with Grandpa Bearskin on a cool spring morning. Listening to the rhythmic lullaby of flowing water and observing the steady persistence of the river shaping stone, she watches river otters and blue herons, and learns how to adapt gracefully to life's obstacles. Together by the riverbank clay patch, they shape a songbird clay whistle, allowing Little Turtle to join her voice with the living harmony of nature and share her reflections with family around the evening hearth fire.

Themes & Nature Connection

Connecting with nature, listening to environmental rhythms, observing adaptation, and discovering inner harmony.

★ Tales of the Wyandotte — Book 3

Little Turtle and the Three Sisters in the Garden

Set within the historical Eastern Woodlands, Little Turtle and the Three Sisters in the Garden follows 10-year-old Little Turtle as she steps into the warm morning sunlight to tend her family’s garden. Guided by the timeless wisdom of her grandmother, Little Turtle discovers the sacred agricultural tradition of the Three Sisters—corn, beans, and squash—and how these companion plants grow, support, and protect one another when planted side by side.

Themes & Stewardship

Traditional Indigenous stewardship, companion planting cooperation, community harmony, and living in balance with the natural world.

About the Author

Travis J. Griffin is a citizen of the Wyandotte Nation in Wyandotte, Oklahoma, a United States Marine Corps veteran, and a professional organizational change manager by trade. Holding a Master’s Degree in Public Administration, Travis brings a deep commitment to community structure, strategic vision, and cultural preservation into all of his creative works.

Pioneering meaningful literature, Travis combines his professional background in structured communication with his heritage to craft values-driven children's literature designed to foster intergenerational dialogue.

Author's Note & Historical Disclaimer

The stories and characters within Tales of the Wyandotte: Little Turtle's Journeys are rooted in the rich history, cultural traditions, and lifeways of the Wyandotte Nation. While historical facts, traditional agricultural practices (such as the Three Sisters companion planting), and cultural elements have been carefully researched and honored, certain narrative events, dialogue, and character interactions have been shaped with creative and editorial liberties to ensure an engaging, imaginative, and age-appropriate reading experience for young readers.
